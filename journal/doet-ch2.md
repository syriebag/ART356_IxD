## Chapter 2: The Psychology of Everyday Actions
Reading through this book was more of a reflection inwardly towards my psychology and, in a way, trying to understand how I process information or output them. This chapter also reminded me of a book called Atomic Habits by James Clear, and how they talked about similar concepts. Most of this journal log will be on reflection than providing examples.

### 1. Subconscious vs Conscious
This concept from the chapter made me really reflect on myself. What things that are just subconscious to me? Should I be more conscious of other things? Though the book talks about design, it helped me be wary of one's mind and actions. 
As I look into my memory reading the book and writing this, there has been conscious activities that have changed to subconscious activities for me. As a noted example from Norman in p41 and 47, driving. Many can relate to this, but the longer and frequent you drive in a known route, the more subconsciously you drive. Funny, but guilty. This made me think how my brain, being accustomed to the route, has been in positive emotional state (p49) where it is relax and calm in a familiar state though the activity, driving, was originally nerve wrecking to me.
Relating it to me as a designer, this made me reflect on how I can make designs intuitive, subconscious, for the users. That is why I maybe have a liking and adherence towards industry standard designs when it comes to interfaces as it proven to be usable for users.
